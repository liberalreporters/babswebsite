Proposed public priorities

The Ogunlade Legislative Blueprint

A Six-Point Agenda for Jobs, Education, Healthcare, Infrastructure, Equity and Accountability

As the 2027 Oyo State House of Assembly election approaches, Ibarapa East is asking what effective representation should look like and how the people should measure the mandate they give. This six-point blueprint sets out how Hon. Babatunde Sunday Ogunlade, APC candidate for Ibarapa East Constituency, intends to approach that responsibility. It is offered as a public standard against which his candidacy, and his work if elected, can be examined.

A message to the people

Beyond the familiar rituals of electoral politics

For Eruwa, Lanlate and the surrounding communities, the answer will depend on more than rallies, endorsements or the size of a crowd. It will depend on the quality of ideas, the credibility of proposed solutions, the willingness to listen, and the ability to keep constituency interests present wherever legislative decisions are made.

Familiarity must never replace accountability. Longstanding relationships do not settle the question of legislative effectiveness, and a new proposition should not be exempt from scrutiny either. This blueprint welcomes the questions voters are right to ask. It does not promise effortless solutions to complex problems. It gives constituents a clear basis for comparing proposals and asking for explanations.

“A representative must be judged not by how often they are seen, but by how seriously they take the people’s interests.”

The six-point agenda

Jobs, education, healthcare, infrastructure, equity and accountability

Each pillar below is a proposed priority for advocacy, consultation and legislative engagement, not a claim of completed delivery.

01

Jobs and economic empowerment

Across Eruwa, Lanlate and neighbouring communities, the local economy runs on farmers, market traders, mechanics, builders, transport operators, technicians and small-business owners. Their ability to grow often depends on financing, infrastructure, vocational opportunities and access to markets. Proposed priorities include:

  • Promote vocational and technical training.
  • Encourage entrepreneurship and practical guidance for young people.
  • Support micro and small enterprises, including traders, artisans and farmers.
  • Advocate access to opportunities that generate sustainable livelihoods.
  • Move the conversation beyond temporary election-season relief.

The role of a legislator: A legislator cannot single-handedly create an employment system or guarantee business success. The office can advocate relevant policies, scrutinise public programmes, support sound budgetary priorities, engage responsible agencies and draw attention to barriers facing local enterprise.

02

Education

Education secures the constituency's future. The quality of learning available to children and young people shapes their employment prospects, independence and ability to contribute to their communities. Proposed priorities include:

  • Advocate improved public school infrastructure.
  • Promote teacher development.
  • Support scholarship opportunities.
  • Assist students from less privileged backgrounds.
  • Give vocational education equal attention alongside academic pathways.

The role of a legislator: A scholarship can help one student, but a sustainable agenda must also consider classrooms, learning materials and school facilities. A legislator can advocate policy, scrutinise education spending and engage education authorities. Each commitment under this pillar is intended to be expressed through identifiable objectives and reported on openly.

03

Healthcare

Healthcare is one of the clearest tests of whether governance responds to ordinary people. For a family facing a medical emergency, the condition of the nearest health facility affects the speed, cost and quality of care. Proposed priorities include:

  • Advocate improved primary healthcare centres, with essential equipment, qualified personnel, medicines and reliable referral arrangements.
  • Support medical outreach initiatives in the communities.
  • Pay particular attention to vulnerable residents who struggle to access essential services.

The role of a legislator: A legislator does not run the public health system, but can raise documented concerns, engage the responsible authorities, examine health spending within the Assembly's remit and push for a fairer distribution of services. Healthcare deserves sustained attention all year, not only when campaigns begin.

04

Infrastructure

Infrastructure matters because of what it enables. A road connects farmers to markets, students to schools and residents to essential services. Reliable electricity supports small enterprise. Safe water improves public health. Proposed priorities include:

  • Document community needs and identify the most pressing priorities for roads, electricity and water.
  • Engage the relevant ministries and agencies.
  • Advocate appropriate budgetary attention.
  • Monitor public commitments within the limits of legislative authority.
  • Give maintenance proper weight, so that public spending is not wasted by neglect.

The measure of success is not the visibility of projects, but whether advocacy produces practical improvements in people's lives and whether the public is told clearly how priorities were set and how progress is going.

05

Equity and fair representation

Different communities have different priorities. One area may be most concerned about roads, another about water, schools, healthcare or markets. A representative must look beyond the most visible communities. Proposed priorities include:

  • Pursue balanced development across the constituency.
  • Engage communities so that overlooked concerns are heard.
  • Consider competing priorities fairly, using transparent criteria.
  • Explain the reasons behind development priorities.
  • Serve every constituent, including those who did not vote for the winner.

Equity does not mean identical resources for every ward. Different communities need different interventions. Fairness means transparent criteria, an honest understanding of relative need and a willingness to explain.

06

Accountability

Campaigns look to the future. The harder test begins after the election, when constituents need to know what their representative is pursuing, which commitments are under consideration, what obstacles have arisen and how their concerns are being handled. Proposed priorities include:

  • Hold regular town hall meetings where residents can raise concerns, ask questions and receive explanations.
  • Maintain ongoing community engagement.
  • Monitor development priorities and report on them publicly.
  • Tell constituents which issues belong to the executive arm of government or other institutions.

Clear project reporting: Updates are intended to distinguish four stages: proposed, approved, under implementation and completed.

Accountability does not mean every problem is solved at once. It means explaining what has been done, what remains outstanding, what constraints exist and what comes next.

Legislative responsibility

What can one legislator deliver?

The Oyo State House of Assembly is part of the state's lawmaking and oversight framework. Members consider bills, examine public finances through the legislative process, contribute to oversight and represent constituency concerns. A legislator does not independently control every government department, infrastructure project, health facility or employment programme. Many outcomes depend on executive decisions, budget approvals, administrative capacity and cooperation among institutions.

Within that framework, a legislator can:

  • Advocate for the constituency's priorities.
  • Sponsor or support relevant legislation.
  • Scrutinise public programmes.
  • Question responsible officials through proper procedures.
  • Ensure local concerns are heard during policy and budget discussions.
  • Keep a structured channel of communication open with constituents.

This blueprint is built around those responsibilities. That connection is what turns a statement of aspiration into a legislative programme.

Experience

Experience applied to public responsibility

Hon. Ogunlade brings political involvement together with professional experience in accounting, business management and administration. These experiences offer useful perspective on financial management, organisational planning, enterprise development and institutional performance. Qualifications alone do not guarantee legislative effectiveness, and voters are right to ask how experience will be applied to the duties of the office. This blueprint invites that scrutiny, and the same standard applies to every candidate.

Standards of service

A mandate for the whole constituency

The APC platform is the political structure through which Hon. Ogunlade seeks the mandate of Ibarapa East, but representation extends beyond party lines. An elected member serves the constituency as a whole, and the concerns of residents do not become irrelevant because they support another party or disagree on particular issues.

A shared task

From promises to a public standard

Constituents should be able to tell apart commitments announced, legislative efforts undertaken, government decisions secured and development outcomes actually delivered. These distinctions protect the public from exaggerated expectations and give a representative a fair way to show progress.

As the campaign continues, this blueprint will be developed with greater practical definition: specific outcomes, the institutions to be engaged, how community priorities will be determined, what information will be shared with constituents and how progress will be assessed.

Ibarapa East deserves representation that listens before it speaks, thinks before it promises, advocates with purpose and stays accountable long after the election is over.

Listen. Think. Advocate. Answer.
